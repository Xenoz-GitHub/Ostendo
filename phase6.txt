Phase 6 — University & Career Ecosystem

Goal:
Expand the platform from an individual student portfolio builder into a student professional identity platform that universities, organizations, and eventually employers can participate in.

The important thing is that the original product remains intact:

A student owns their portfolio and controls their professional identity.

Universities become an additional layer—not the owner of the student's personal website.

1. University Accounts

Universities can create an organization account.

For example:

University of Example
        │
        ├── Students
        ├── Programs
        ├── Departments
        └── Events

A university administrator can manage the institution's presence on the platform.

2. University Verification

Universities should go through an organization verification process.

Potentially:

University
   ↓
Apply for organization account
   ↓
Verification
   ↓
Approved
   ↓
University dashboard

This prevents random users from pretending to represent a university.

3. University Branding

A university can have its own branded area.

For example:

University of Example

Institution name

But the branding should remain professional.

No giant gradients or flashy university landing pages.

4. Student Affiliation

A student can associate their portfolio with a university.

For example:

Computer Science
University of Example
2024–2028

The university can verify that affiliation if appropriate.

The student's personal portfolio remains theirs.

5. Verified Student Badge

A verified affiliation could appear on a portfolio:

✓ University of Example

This should mean something.

It shouldn't simply be a decorative badge.

The verification system needs to clearly define what is being verified.

7. Student Privacy

This is extremely important.

A university should not automatically gain access to everything on a student's account.

Students should control:

Public portfolio

Contact information
Projects
Resume

Personal links

University administrators should only see information they are explicitly authorized to see.

Decline

Universities could provide optional templates.

For example:

27. University Domains

Eventually universities could have:

students.university.edu

or potentially student subdomains.

For example:

crystal.students.university.edu

This would be a much later enterprise feature because domain infrastructure and institutional requirements become more complex.

28. Organization Roles

University accounts need role-based permissions.

For example:

University
│
├── Owner
├── Administrator
├── Program Manager
├── Department Manager
└── Viewer

Each role gets appropriate permissions.

This becomes important once multiple university employees use the platform.

29. Audit Logs

At this scale, important administrative actions should be recorded.

For example:

Administrator
   ↓
Verified student
   ↓
March 12, 2027

or:

Program administrator changed program settings.

This improves accountability and security.

30. Organization Data Isolation

This becomes a major architectural requirement.

One university must never be able to access another university's private data.

Conceptually:

University A
 ├── Students
 ├── Programs
 └── Projects

University B
 ├── Students
 ├── Programs
 └── Projects

Authorization must be enforced server-side.

Never rely only on UI restrictions.

32. Multi-Tenant Architecture

The platform should logically separate organization data.

Conceptually:

Platform
│
├── University A
│    ├── Students
│    ├── Programs
│    └── Events
│
├── University B
│    ├── Students
│    ├── Programs
│    └── Events
│
└── Individual users

This needs to be designed carefully before large-scale university adoption.

33. Vercel Plan

Vercel can continue hosting the web application.

Users
   ↓
Vercel
   ↓
Application
   ↓
Prisma
   ↓
MySQL

As traffic grows, we should stop thinking only about:

“Can this fit within the free tier?”

and start thinking:

“What infrastructure does the actual traffic justify?”

The $0 strategy is excellent for validation.

It shouldn't become a constraint once the product has meaningful usage.

34. PWA

The student dashboard remains an installable PWA.

University administrators can also use the dashboard through the browser/PWA.

The public-facing pages remain optimized websites.

35. What Phase 6 Does NOT Mean

We're still not automatically building a giant LinkedIn competitor.

Avoid immediately adding:

❌ Feed
❌ Likes
❌ Followers
❌ Viral posts
❌ Public messaging
❌ Generic social networking
❌ Endless scrolling
❌ Engagement algorithms

The platform should remain centered around:

Student → Portfolio → Projects → Education → Career
